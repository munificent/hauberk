Here's an idea to address some of the stuff at the end of the previous note on
player progression.

We separate out skills and abilities more completely.

## Skills

A skill is a thing you can spend experience to level up. Every skill supports a
range of levels and has an incrementally better effect the more you level it up.
Skills can only have passive effects or indirectly affect the behavior of other
operations. You don't "use" a skill.

You spend experience to level up a skill, including leveling it from zero to
one.

Class determines the maximum level you can have in each skill (which may be
zero) and the experience cost to raise each skill.

Equipment can boost skill levels. I'm not sure if it can boost beyond the max,
but probably.

Skills are things like:

* Weapon masteries
* Slays
* Proficiency at various spell schools/arcana
* Backstabbing and other rogue skills

## Abilities

An ability is an earnable capability that lets the hero perform a new action.
Casting a spell and doing an axe swing are abilities. Unlike skills, abilities
are not leveled. You can either do it or not do it.

Abilities are earned by spending experience. In addition, each ability has
requirements that must be met before it can be unlocked. Often this is a
certain minimum level in some skill, but can be other things like having a
certain kind of weapon equipped. For deity-related skills, the requirements may
be that you have *not* done a certain thing, like wield a blade.

Even after you have spent experience to learn an ability, it is temporarily
unavailable if the requirements are not currently met. It will become available
again when the requirements are met again.

## Spell power

Spells are abilities, so you can either cast them or not. But as much as
possible, each spell's effect will depend on a "spell power". This is based on
the character's intellect plus the skill level of the "arcana" or "spell school"
skill that the spell falls under.

So a Raise Dead spell will get more powerful if you raise your intellect, if you
train your Necromancy skill, or if you equip something that boosts intellect or
Necromancy.

The arcana are tags similar to how breeds are organized, so a spell can be in
multiple arcana at the same time. A Fire Brand spell might be in both Fire and
Enchanting arcana.

## Race and class powers

In addition to how they tweak stat and skill caps and experience rates, races
and classes also provide powers. Powers can provide passive effects, sort of
like skills, or access to unique abilities.

For a warrior-like class, one power we could possibly give it is the ability to
automatically train weapon skills by using the weapon, and likewise auto-train
slay skills by killing the right kinds of things.

Each race has a couple of powers as does each class. These should mechanically
interesting, unique capabilities, not just minor tweaks. The goal is that all
combinations of race and class are worth considering.

## Dual class

Farther down the road, if I want even more character creation options, I could
probably support dual class characters that give you the powers of two classes
and merge their skill caps, with some sort of penalty to not overpower them.

## Combinatorial character creation

There is a lot of mechanics in play here, and whether it works well or not
hinges a lot of coming up with good race powers, class powers, skills, and
abilities. But overall, it feels pretty good.

In particular, the distinction between skill (passive, leveled) and ability
(active, unleveled) I think does a good job of modeling how spells should work
while also being expandable to non-spell things (axe sweep, deity granted
powers, etc.)

Here's the summary:

*   Skills are leveled by spending experience and provide some passive benefit.

*   Abilities are unlocked by spending experience and meeting some other
    ability-specific requirement and provide access to a new action the hero
    can perform.

*   In addition to tweaking stat cap and experience cost, each race defines a
    couple of unique race powers that only members of that race have. Powers
    can be passive (like a pre-learned skill) or active (like a pre-learned
    ability).

*   Class defines the maximum level of each skill and the experience cost to
    level it. Some skills are shared across multiple classes while others are
    likely to be specific to one class.

*   Each class also defines a couple of unique class powers that only members of
    that class have. Again, they can be active or passive.

*   Equipment can boost skill level which may in turn grant access to abilities
    that are gated by skill level.

*   Maybe support dual class characters at some point.

Abilities aren't directly tied to classes so can be shared across multiple. Many
will have a skill level requirement so will have some affinity to certain
classes, but others may not be. (In fact, if I wanted to separate deity worship
completely from class, I could have granted powers be abilities whose
requirements don't relate to skills at all.)

Skill caps are affected by class, but multiple classes can have non-zero caps
in the same skill, so I can get some skill reuse across classes. At the same
time, by having different skill caps, class directly affects the skills a hero
can have and through ability requirements, the abilities they can learn.

Each combination of race and class starts a hero with a unique combination of
capabilities. They further refine by choosing which stats, skills, and abilities
to invest in.

Of course, the hard part is coming up with the races, race powers, classes,
class powers, skills, abilities, and ability requirements.
